What Luciano Does For You

Luciano is the one voice you actually deal with. Fourteen specialists work your account overnight; Luciano reads everything they found and hands you a single plain-English briefing in the morning — what changed, what it cost, and the one thing worth doing about it.

He is the reason a fifteen-agent team doesn't turn into fifteen dashboards. Every finding, from every agent, funnels through him into one ranked list of decisions.

Everything Luciano Can Do1 briefing a day

Your morning briefing

  • Writes a fresh briefing every morning — leads in the last 24 hours versus the day before, and how many were qualified
  • Names your best and worst performing ad by cost-per-lead over the trailing two weeks
  • Leads with the single most urgent open issue across all fourteen other agents
  • Folds in what's waiting for your approval and any new tour bookings
  • If a briefing ever fails to generate, that failure is recorded and flagged rather than passed off as a quiet day

The decision board

  • Sorts every agent's findings into six plain areas: money, leads, creative, targeting, tracking, and presence
  • Gives each area one verdict and one top action, so you're never comparing fourteen severity scores
  • Shows money at stake as a worst-single-case figure, never a padded total — it deliberately refuses to add findings together
  • Runs on fixed logic, not a language model, so the same data always produces the same board

Answering your questions

  • Answers questions about your account in the dashboard, grounded in your own findings, approvals, run history and connection status
  • Answers "is this agent actually working?" straight from the run ledger, with no guesswork
  • Puts an "ask why" button on findings so you can get the reasoning behind any single recommendation
  • Redacts internal system details out of every reply before you see it

Learning your business

  • Runs a knowledge desk where you teach him the things data can't show — your catchment, your seasons, your real competitors
  • Drafts follow-up questions each night based on gaps in what he knows about you
  • Runs the onboarding interview that configures the rest of the team

Reports that leave the dashboard

  • Daily approval digest by email so nothing sits in the queue unseen
  • Weekly performance report every Monday morning
  • Monthly client report you can print to PDF, plus share links you can send to a partner

What Runs On Its Own — And What Waits For You

▶ Runs automatically

  • Morning briefing, generated on a schedule
  • Decision board, recalculated on every dashboard load
  • Daily approval digest email
  • Monday weekly report
  • Nightly knowledge questions

⏸ Waits for your approval

  • Everything else — Luciano proposes nothing that executes on its own
  • He routes work into the approval queue; he cannot approve it for you
  • He has no ability to change an ad, a page, or a listing

Where Luciano Sits In The Swarm

Your agents don't sit in a chat room talking to each other — they share one workspace. Every night, on a fixed schedule, each agent writes what it found onto a shared findings board and into a single approval queue. Every other agent can read that board, and so can you. A few agents also hand each other specific intelligence directly. The result is one queue of decisions instead of fifteen inboxes.

You. Luciano is the last stop before a human — he is where the swarm stops being technical.

Worth being precise about one thing: Luciano reports, he does not command. He doesn't dispatch the other agents or trigger their runs — they work on their own schedule and he reads what they wrote. That's a deliberate design choice. It means no agent is ever waiting on him, and a problem with Luciano can never stop the specialists from working.

Straight Talk

Luciano's briefing and decision board run on fixed logic — same data in, same answer out. That's on purpose: the numbers you're shown are never a language model's paraphrase of your account.

The conversational "ask Luciano" layer is the one part that uses a language model, and it is capped: a limited number of messages a day, and it can only see data your account already has.
