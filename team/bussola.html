What Bussola Does For You

Every ad platform grades its own homework. Facebook counts a lead; Google counts a conversion; your analytics counts something else entirely. Bussola is the agent whose whole job is to sit between them and tell you which number to believe.

She is also the swarm's referee — the only agent with the authority to tell the ad agents to be quiet.

Everything Bussola Can Do7 nightly checks

Checking the platforms against reality

  • Compares what each ad platform reports against what your analytics actually recorded, channel by channel
  • Classifies the *direction* of a gap rather than jumping to a conclusion — it deliberately refuses to declare "your tracking is broken" just because two numbers differ
  • Flags channels where the divergence is big enough to matter
  • Puts Google, Meta and your analytics side by side without averaging them or picking a favourite — and explains the gaps that are structural rather than broken

Catching tracking failures

  • Raises a critical alert when real traffic arrives and records zero conversions — the silent killer
  • Flags analytics data that has gone stale
  • Flags a data source that quietly stopped reporting while everything else kept flowing
  • Flags an outsized share of "direct" traffic, which almost always means campaign tagging has been lost somewhere

Where the money is going

  • Flags any channel that took real spend or real traffic and produced no recorded conversions
  • Flags a paid channel whose cost per conversion is far worse than your blended average

Reading your traffic

  • A rolling four-week traffic picture by channel
  • A weekly funnel view labelled with how much to trust each stage
  • Page-level verdicts: which pages convert, which don't, and which simply don't have enough traffic to judge

Where Bussola Sits In The Swarm

Your agents don't sit in a chat room talking to each other — they share one workspace. Every night, on a fixed schedule, each agent writes what it found onto a shared findings board and into a single approval queue. Every other agent can read that board, and so can you. A few agents also hand each other specific intelligence directly. The result is one queue of decisions instead of fifteen inboxes.

Annunci and Faccia directly — as a gate on what they're allowed to tell you.

This is the clearest example of the agents genuinely affecting each other. Before Annunci or Faccia hands you conversion-based advice, they ask Bussola whether the conversion data for that channel holds up. If it doesn't, the advice is suppressed and labelled rather than presented as fact. It's deliberately built to fail safe: if Bussola can't reach a verdict, the other agents carry on rather than going silent.
